# Enabling Scalable Evaluation of Bias Patterns in Medical LLMs

### Abstract
The rapid integration of large language models in medical applications (Med LLMs) has raised critical questions about fairness and
potential biased behaviors. To pave the way for the responsible and impactful deployment of Med LLMs, rigorous evaluation is a
key prerequisite. Due to the high complexity and variability across medical scenarios, existing work in this domain has primarily
relied on manually crafted datasets for bias evaluation. In this study, we present a new method to scale up such bias evaluations by
generating evaluation cases based on rigorous medical evidence. We use knowledge graphs and medical ontologies, coupled with
a base LLM in a generative pipeline, to generate red-teaming evaluation scenarios. Such scenarios are commonly used to evaluate
Med LLMs and often include medical question-answering cases, in which changes in an LLM’s response to the same question with
only a sensitive attribute flipped (e.g., a patient with vs. without insurance) can indicate unfair behavior. We specifically target the
challenges of domain-specific bias characterization (e.g., in prevention vs. diagnosis), hallucination, and the dependencies between
health outcomes and sensitive attributes (e.g., gender-dependent diseases). Through extensive experiments, we show that the test
cases generated by our proposed method are reliable and can effectively reveal bias patterns in Med LLMs. Additionally, we publish a
bias evaluation dataset, providing a comprehensive platform for testing and improving the fairness of medical LLMs.
